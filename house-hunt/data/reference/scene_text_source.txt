# checked by scripts/check_scene_text.py. Edit here, never in the CSVs. docs/SCENE_TEXT_NOTES.md describes the system.
#
# House rules (copy edit of 2026-11-16; the checker enforces most of them):
#  - Plain sentences that make sense on first read. No personification, no jokes that need decoding, no noun standing
#    in for a claim ("the cost is the map"), no "where the car sleeps", no "home" for her parents' house.
#  - Numbers are tokens, never typed (see the builder's docstring). A claim without a number carries a hidden test.
#  - Counts, prices and minutes to her parents come from the page's figure strip ({pg_n}, {pg_mid}, {drive}, {p_places},
#    {p_allin}, {p_drive} ...), so the sentence and the strip above it cannot disagree.
#  - "your parents" in sentences; "apartment" for a unit; "building"; "neighborhood"; "experience". No "all-in" without
#    its explanation: write "a month, everything included".
#  - Residents are paraphrased as "Residents say / like / praise / call / wish ..." and the block then needs a voices:
#    line naming the themes. A number quoted from a local rule needs a rules: line.
#  - A neighborhood block: host_intro (the "About <name>" lead: what is different about THIS place, then the catch;
#    its first sentence is also the neighborhood's line on the experience page), scene (daily life: on foot, the dog,
#    the car), good (one sentence), watch (one sentence). Write less when the data gives nothing distinctive.
#  - {b_toll} is always worded "about $N round trip with a New York E-ZPass".

# ================================================================== THE SIX EXPERIENCES
# The key stays "=== year: ... Year"; display: is what the page prints. For each experience: who it suits, a normal
# day, a Saturday night, Sunday with her parents, and what she gives up, with no fact said twice.
#   tagline (4-14 words) and subhead: one plain line each.
#   host_intro: the "About this experience" lead, 2 or 3 sentences: who it suits, what life is like, what she gives up.
#   points: 4 to 6 `icon | text` joined by ;; (exactly one `warning`). The strip above already shows the price, the
#           drive and the counts, so a point repeats one of those only when it is the catch.
#   moment1-3: a weekday / Saturday night / Sunday with her parents, at most 35 words each. The first clause of
#           moment1 becomes the headline of the collapsed "A day in this experience" row, so it must stand alone.
#   promise (one sentence), catch (one or two).
# p_ tokens are the figure strip's own values; u_ tokens are medians or shares over the apartments shown.

# An experience that shows fewer than 8 apartments is written as what it is (independent check 4, D11): a short note
# that names its neighborhoods and gives each one's own figures, with no "typical", no percentage and no "most".
# Its draft is the block marked "@small"; "@full" is used from 8 apartments up. {@nj:teaneck drive} reads a
# neighborhood's own figure; {!hood_names==...} stops the build when the neighborhoods change, so the names typed in
# the prose are always the ones on the page.
# A claim about an experience ("close to your parents", "no tolls", "you drive to dinner", "small apartments", "a
# garage") is tested by the checker against the apartments shown and against every neighborhood shown under it: it
# must hold for 75% of the apartments with no neighborhood contradicting it, or name the exceptions ("except in
# Stamford", "the Newark ones"), or be limited to named places ("From Brooklyn and Queens ..."), or say "most".
