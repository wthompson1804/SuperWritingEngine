# checked by scripts/check_scene_text.py. Edit here, never in the CSVs. docs/SCENE_TEXT_NOTES.md describes the system.

# House rules (copy edit of 2026-11-16; the checker enforces most of them):
#  - Plain sentences that make sense on first read. No personification, no jokes that need decoding, no noun standing
#    in for a claim ("the cost is the map"), no "where the car sleeps", no "home" for her parents' house.
#  - Numbers are tokens, never typed (see the builder's docstring). A claim without a number carries a hidden test.
#  - Counts, prices and minutes to her parents come from the page's figure strip ({pg_n}, {pg_mid}, {drive}, {p_places},
#    {p_allin}, {p_drive} ...), so the sentence and the strip above it cannot disagree.
#  - "your parents" in sentences; "apartment" for a unit; "building"; "neighborhood"; "experience". No "all-in" without
#    its explanation: write "a month, everything included".
#  - Residents are paraphrased as "Residents say / like / praise / call / wish ..." and the block then needs a voices:
#    line naming the themes. A number quoted from a local rule needs a rules: line.
#  - A neighborhood block: host_intro (the "About <name>" lead: what is different about THIS place, then the catch;
#    its first sentence is also the neighborhood's line on the experience page), scene (daily life: on foot, the dog,
#    the car), good (one sentence), watch (one sentence). Write less when the data gives nothing distinctive.
#  - {b_toll} is always worded "about $N round trip with a New York E-ZPass".
