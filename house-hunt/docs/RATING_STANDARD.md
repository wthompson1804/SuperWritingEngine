# Rating standard for buildings outside New York City

Generated by `scripts/rating_standard.py` on 2026-11-15 from: `ratings_round1.csv`, `ratings_batch_11.csv`, `ratings_batch_7.csv`, `ratings_batch_8.csv`, `nyc_records_flags.csv`, `avoid_buildings.csv`. Rerun the script when more `ratings_batch_*.csv` files arrive; every number below is recomputed.

## Bottom line

- **Ratings cannot stand in for city records.** Among NYC buildings that have both a rating and city records, the star rating does not separate record-based avoid buildings from the rest (AUC 0.51, 95% interval 0.32 to 0.7; 0.5 means no relationship; 13 flagged of 62).
- **The ratings are thin and old.** 111 of 291 checked buildings show any rating. Outside NYC the usual source is the Google figure that Zumper displays, which Zumper last refreshed a median of 21 months ago; 0 outside-NYC buildings had a readable review from the last 6 months.
- **So the rule below is a screen, not a verdict.** It can raise a flag ("pause") and, rarely, an avoid. It cannot prove a suburban building clean: "ok" on an old rating says nothing about the last 6 months, and most buildings land in "not enough data".

## 4. Proposed rule for buildings outside NYC

Applied per building, in this order. Review counts refer to the single best source; recent counts are review texts dated in the last 6 months, summed over sources.

| Outcome | Condition |
|---|---|
| **avoid** | 3 or more negative reviews in the last 6 months (the plan's existing management test, now counted in a structured way); **or** a rating at or below the low line on 20+ reviews, not older than 12 months, together with 2 or more negative reviews in the last 6 months |
| **pause** | 1 or 2 negative reviews in the last 6 months; **or** a rating at or below the pause line on 5+ reviews |
| **ok** | rating above the pause line on 20+ reviews with no negative review in the last 6 months; or 3+ recent review texts, none negative. Marked STALE when the rating is over 12 months old and no recent text was readable |
| **not_enough_data** | no rating; or fewer than 5 reviews; or 5 to 19 reviews with fewer than 3 recent texts (too few to clear a building) |

Lines per source (stars out of 5):

| Source | Low line | Pause line | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google (via Zumper or own-site widget) | 3.7 | 4.0 | pause = lower quartile of outside-NYC Google ratings with 20+ reviews (n=27); low = the percentile equal to the NYC record-based avoid base rate (15%), at least 0.3 below the pause line |
| Openigloo | 2.0 | 2.5 | lower quartile of NYC Openigloo ratings; not used outside NYC |
| Apartment List, other | 2.5 | 3.5 | judgment; these almost never reach the review minimum |

**Why 20 and 5 reviews.** Star ratings within one building typically spread about 1.3 to 1.5 stars. The average of 5 reviews is then uncertain by more than a star either way (95%), which cannot tell a 3.5 building from a 4.5 one; at 20 reviews it is about 0.6 stars, enough to place a building below or above the pause line. Below 5, a rating is one or two people's experience and is reported, not judged.

**Why the last 6 months carry the avoid.** Will's management rule judges the last 6 months. An all-time star average cannot show a recent change of manager or a new outage problem, so a low average alone only earns "pause".

## 5. Weaknesses

1. **Not validated.** The NYC calibration does not show that ratings predict record-based problems, so the lines are distribution-based (bottom quartile), not accuracy-based. Treat "pause" as "ask on the tour", not as evidence.
2. **Stale Google figures.** Zumper's copies date from late 2024 to early 2025. A building can have changed manager since. Google itself is off limits to us (Google Maps robots rules), so the live figure and the newest reviews must be read by hand on a phone.
3. **Selection in the texts.** Zumper shows the eight highest-rated reviews. Zero negatives among them means nothing; the rule therefore never counts them as "recent, none negative" unless they are actually dated in the last 6 months.
4. **Different instruments inside and outside NYC.** No building has both an Openigloo and a meaningful Google rating, so the two scales cannot be cross-walked directly; the per-source quartile is the only link.
5. **Car break-ins and elevator outages are not visible in ratings.** Reviews rarely mention them, and star averages do not move on them. Outside NYC these two deal-breakers rest on dated reports and, where they exist, official records (`data/evidence/non_nyc_records.csv`: NJ housing-inspection reports for New Jersey buildings, Jersey City police incidents).
6. **Small numbers.** Every figure here rests on tens of buildings. Rerun after round 2.
7. **Keyword tagging.** Serious-issue tags come from keyword matching with a simple negation guard; Openigloo has no per-review stars, so its "negative" count means a serious issue named under Cons.

## 6. What would make this a real standard

- Someone reads the live Google listing for each outside-NYC finalist and records the current rating, count, and the newest 10 reviews with dates (ten minutes per building).
- For New Jersey, use the state housing-inspection reports as the record-based test (open violations at the last inspection, repeat reinspections), as NYC uses HPD.
- Keep "no avoid flag outside NYC" worded as "not checked against records", not "clean".
