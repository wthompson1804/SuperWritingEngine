Basic Rules of Glen Cove Beaches
No dogs are permitted at any facility at any time.
Flotation devices and ball playing are prohibited.
Alcohol is not permitted at any time.
Bathe in designated swim areas only and only when lifeguards are on duty.
When you hear the whistle, look at the lifeguard’s chair. They may want your attention.
No diving off of floats or piers.
Rollerblades, skateboards, bicycles and scooters are not permitted beyond parking areas.
Smoking, vaping, marijuana use, and all other smoking products are prohibited.
Help keep our beaches clean; please dispose of garbage in receptacles provided.
Who may use the beaches?
Only Glen Cove residents may use Pryibil Beach and Crescent Beach. Residents of Glen Cove and Locust Valley may use Morgan Park. The beaches are monitored with beach gate attendants from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day. Attendants check for proof of residency. You must have your driver’s license and registration with you showing your Glen Cove address, to be issued a beach permit. Please note, this applies year-round.
Beaches are now open with lifeguards on duty 10 am-6 pm, weather permitting. Please sign up for alerts from the city in the event of beach closures.

When and Where Stickers Are Issued
Beach Parking Stickers and placards are issued at beach locations during the season (Memorial Day through Labor Day) and at the Recreation Department at City Hall. Residents may apply online or in person.
What You Need to Apply
All applicants must provide current vehicle registration and proof of residency. Acceptable proof includes a driver’s license, lease, or utility bill with a local address.
NYS DMV regulations require vehicle registration addresses to be updated within 10 days of relocating.
Stickers and Placards
Stickers are issued when residency and vehicle registration are valid. Placards may be issued if vehicle registration is not local or for approved special circumstances. Stickers and placards are non-transferable, and any alteration renders them invalid.
Enforcement
The Glen Cove Police Department conducts license plate identification checks at local beaches. Non-resident vehicles may be subject to tickets and fines.

Morgan Memorial Park
Morgan Memorial Park is a cherished 40-acre park located in Glen Cove, New York, offering scenic waterfront views and lush green spaces for the community to enjoy. The park holds a rich history, beginning with J.P. Morgan Jr.’s dedication of the land to honor his late wife, Jane Norton Morgan, following her passing in 1925.
Compiled for the 350th Anniversary of the Founding of Glen Cove and the 100th Anniversary Celebrating the City of Glen Cove. Compiled by Dave Nieri, 2017:
In 1927, J.P. Morgan Jr. purchased land at the western end of The Landing, where the Pavilion Hotel and later “The Hall” once stood. This area included a number of homes, and Morgan acquired additional properties, including West Street, to amass the necessary acreage. Both manor houses and cottages were demolished, and the land was beautifully landscaped to create what is now Morgan Memorial Park, a serene harborside space dedicated to the memory of his wife.
Officially opened on July 4, 1932, Morgan Memorial Park was given to the City of Glen Cove through a 999-year lease, a generous and unheard-of gesture at the time. This park is believed to be Glen Cove’s first public park, preceded only by the Harbor Beach Pavilion near Glen Cove Creek (1902-1922), which provided the community with a bathing beach and changing facilities. The park is available for the enjoyment of residents from both Glen Cove and Locust Valley, where Jane Morgan’s family, the Pennoyers, hailed from.
The park is known for hosting a variety of community events and celebrations, including picnics, swimming, fireworks displays, wedding photo sessions, and concerts. Since 1959, the Morgan Park Summer Music Festival has delighted visitors with free Sunday evening concerts, adding to the park's cultural significance.
*****
Recent Renovations and Ongoing Projects:
In addition to its historical and cultural importance, Morgan Memorial Park has undergone significant renovations in recent years. Under the leadership of Mayor Pamela Panzenbeck, the park saw a major overhaul in 2022, which included the repair of its crumbling seawall, stairs, and bathrooms, as well as the paving of the Morgan Memorial Walk. These improvements were essential in preserving the park for future generations.
On October 1, 2024, construction began on the rehabilitation of the Morgan Park Pavilions.
Morgan Memorial Park remains a treasured part of Glen Cove, offering a tranquil retreat from city life while providing space for residents and visitors to come together for recreation, relaxation, and community events. Whether you’re enjoying a summer concert, a quiet walk along the shore, or a picnic by the water, the park continues to be a vital part of the community’s history and daily life.
Location:
Germaine Street, off western end of Landing Road. (Glen Cove and Locust Valley residents only)
How to get to Morgan Memorial Park
At the Glen Cove Fire House, turn right onto Brewster Street. At first light, make a left onto Mill Hill Road (take the fork going UPHILL). At the top of the hill bear left onto a street called The Place. Make the third left onto McLoughlin Street (you will see a sign for Morgan Park there). Follow McLoughlin Street almost to the end, and make a right onto Germaine Street. The entrance to the parking lot will be on the left.

Crescent Beach
Bathing beach, outdoor shower, restrooms, limited parking.
Although Crescent Beach is Glen Cove’s smallest public beach (Glen Cove residents only), it commands a picturesque view of Hempstead Harbor. Nestled between the former estates of millionaires such as Standard Oil president Herbert L. Pratt and steamboat magnate Lewis Luckenbach, the 2.5 acre beach is usually one of the more placid shoreline spots during the summer months.
Parking is limited; restrooms are available; no concession stand. Bathing water quality is regularly monitored by the Nassau County Department of Health.
How to get there:
At the Glen Cove Fire House, make a right onto Brewster Street. At the fourth light, make a left onto Dosoris Lane. Make the second left onto Woolsey Avenue. Turn right on the fourth street on the right hand side (Crescent Beach Road. Travel north to the end of the road. Please note that the road will curve to the left in front of Webb Institute. (Glen Cove residents only)

Big Ralph Park
Community Youth Garden ReWild Grant
On Veteran’s Day 2013, the City rededicated Big Ralph Park in honor of veteran “Big Ralph” Mastalio.
“Big Ralph” Mastalio bravely served in both World War I and World War II, earning the Bronze Star and Purple Heart for his valor. He also dedicated 32 years to his community as a volunteer firefighter with the Glen Cove Fire Department.
Years ago, the monument honoring him, placed by the Sons of Italy, was removed, leaving a gap in the park’s rich history.
Since then, the park has undergone revitalization, which included expanding the Youth Bureau’s Summer Youth Employment Program (SYEP) community garden, as well as the City of Glen Cove community garden. Enhancements included adding a greenhouse, installing a flagpole, and placing new benches. Most importantly, the rock bearing one of the original plaques dedicated to Ralph Mastalio has been returned to its rightful spot in the park.
Community Youth Garden Volunteers
This garden, which started in 2009, is a project that teaches young people about sustainability, healthy eating, and building a connection with nature.
The garden is also recognized as a Monarch Waystation, helping provide a critical habitat for monarch butterflies. A huge thank you to ReWild Long Island for their generous grant, which made this possible!
In 2024, the Youth Community Garden was a recipient of the
ReWild Long Island
Community Garden free plants initiative. ReWild encourages these initiatives by providing consulting services, plants, and materials at no cost.

City Boat Ramp
Concrete boat launching facility.
Location:
Garvies Point Road
The Glen Cove Boat Ramp is a concrete boat launching facility set on a four-acre parcel at the western terminus of Garvies Point Road. A permit is required.
Link for City Boat Ramp Rates
Link for Boat Ramp Specials
Boat ramp permits are only available at the entrance to the ramp on Garvies Point Road. Weekend and holiday hours are 9 am to 5 pm, and weekday hours are from 8 am to 4 pm. All hours are subject to foul weather closures. When purchasing a seasonal permit, you must have your boat trailer and registration with you.
Commercial haulers may purchase permits at the ramp or through the Parks & Recreation office if multiple carriers from the same hauler will be launching boats.
How to get to City Boat Ramp
Make a left at Glen Cove Fire house onto Glen Cove Avenue. At the first light, make a right onto Charles Street. Follow Charles Street until it intersects Herb Hill Road and name a left onto Herb Hill Road. At the end of Herb Hill Rd (a T intersection) make a left onto Garvies Point Road. Follow Garvies Point Road all the way to the end to the boat ramp.

Transformation of Dennis Brian Murray Park
Dennis Brian Murray Park has undergone a major transformation!
The former Leech Circle Park, perched on a wooded hillside overlooking downtown Glen Cove, was one of the most underutilized recreational facilities in the City of Glen Cove. In the fall of 2003, the park was formally rededicated as Dennis Brian Murray Park, named in honor of a young Glen Cove man killed in action during the Vietnam War.
Demolition of deteriorated playing courts began in late 2002. Despite being hampered to by an exceptionally rainy summer, construction of the first phase of the park was completed in September 2003.
