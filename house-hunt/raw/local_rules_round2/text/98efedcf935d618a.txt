Winter 2026 - Winterberry
Winterberry (Ilex verticillata) is a deciduous native shrub with beautiful, bright red berries which last into mid to late winter, until eaten by birds such as robins, bluebirds, cardinals and waxwings, as well as small mammals. Winterberry shrubs are not commonly browsed by deer, making them a great choice for planting in Princeton!
These native members of the holly family are dioecious, which means that plants bear either male or female flowers, but not both.  To be sure to get the beautiful red berries for which the shrub is named, it is important to have a male pollinator planted within about 50 feet of the female bushes. One male plant can be used to pollinate between 5-15 females.
Winterberries tend to be low maintenance and do best in moist acidic soil with good drainage.  The species can grow 10 to 15 feet tall, but many named cultivars are considerably shorter.  They can be used as foundation plantings, in rain gardens, and in pollinator gardens.  The flowers, which bloom in late spring, are tiny, and the pollinators are equally small.  Since the plants bloom on last year’s growth, careful pruning is necessary to ensure berry production in the fall.  When buying winterberries from a nursery, be sure to match your female bushes with a male which blooms at the same time.
