Community Park North
For the eighth article in our monthly Open Space Showcase, we’ll explore
Community Park North
, a 71-acre forested area
,
which is located north of Bayard Lane (206)/Mountain Avenue and west of Cherry Hill Road. It is sited within a continuous complex of more than 400 acres of preserved open space just east of
Billy Johnson Mountain Lakes Preserve
with the historic
Tusculum
estate to the north.
A 13-acre section of
Community Park North
was rededicated as
Pettoranello Gardens
in 1992, named after Princeton’s Italian sister-town and partially maintained by the
Princeton Pettoranello Sister City Foundation
. The easiest access to the Gardens is via a paved pathway from the main parking lot on Mountain Avenue. Sheltered by a pine-lined berm from the Bayard Lane traffic, benches along the path invite visitors to take in the view of the Community Park Pond which frequently hosts flocks of geese and sunbathing turtles. A small amphitheater is also located here which can be booked for events. The purple unpaved ‘Nature trail’ leads around the northern shore of the pond from where a glimpse of a Seward Johnson sculpture may be had, which is hidden among foliage on a small island.
After crossing a bridge and the grassy sewer easement, one enters a forest which is quite different from the remainder of the adjacent Preserve: In contrast to the generally broadleaf dominated woodlands, this is an area populated largely by Eastern white pine and non-native Norway Spruce, which are believed to have been planted in the early 1960s, shortly before the land was acquired by Princeton for a park. Many of the trees have fallen in recent storms, creating openings in the canopy that have quickly been occupied by invasive species such as Japanese stiltgrass, Oriental bittersweet, multiflora rose, and Japanese honeysuckle.
The scent of pines, sounds of buzzing cicadas, and the occasional chirping chipmunk skittering in the undergrowth creates an immersive sensory experience that is quite reinvigorating. At the junction of the white and yellow trails, a ‘wolf pine’ can be discovered, its’ growth form is characteristic of a tree that was planted in an open area where abundant light and space allowed branches to spread and reach in all directions to take advantage of the open sun. Subsequent infestation by the pine weevil then caused the branches to grow upwards rather than sideways, resulting in the ‘multi-trunk’ appearance.
There are several trail options to experience
Community Park North
thanks to the interconnectedness of open space in this area. A walk can easily made longer by incorporating the fields of
Tusculum
, the boulder fields of J
ohn Witherspoon Woods
or the lakes of
Billy Johnson Mountain Lakes Preserve
. A trail map of the area can be found
here
.
Community Park North
and
Pettoranello Gardens
can be reached via a network of foot and cycle paths from anywhere within the municipal center. The main trailhead and parking for the paved path and the purple trail, which also offers restrooms, can be accessed from Mountain Avenue, while limited parking may also be available in the parking lot of the Unitarian Universalist Church, where the trailhead for the yellow trail can be found.
