Winter 2025 - Barberry
There are two species of this invasive plant currently found in the US: Japanese Barberry (
Berberis thunbergia
), and Common Barberry
Berberis vulgaris).
Both are thorny shrubs that are now on everyone’s “do not plant” list, and are illegal to plant in several states, including nearby Pennsylvania.  Barberries were introduced to North America in the 1800’s as an ornamental and landscape plant. Grown for their interesting foliage, deer resistance and adaptability, barberry has been discovered naturalizing in almost every state east of the Mississippi, and as far west as Washington. Barberry spreads by seeds and is a prolific seed producer. Birds eat the seeds, facilitating spread to new areas. Armed with impressive one-inch thorns, this plant is highly deer-resistant.  However, there are many thornless native replacements, also deer resistant, including Virginia Sweetspire (
Itea virginica
), Winterberry (
Ilex verticillata
), and Summersweet (
Clethra alnifolia
) to name a few.
This is the last article in the PEC’s series on invasive plants. In 2025 our focus will turn to native plants which thrive in New Jersey gardens, many of which are also seldom browsed by deer.
